Mentoring Programme for Young Scientists

The BSC Bioinfo4Women International Mentoring Programme for Young Scientists aims to provide young scientists, particularly women, with the skills and knowledge needed for future leadership positions in STEM. Mentees are matched with international, high-profile researchers who provide guidance, share experience, and act as role models.

The pilot programme started in early 2022 with a one-year duration. The mentoring relationship is framed as a two-way learning partnership that values differences and supports developmental conversations, experience sharing, and role modelling.

Pilot Objectives (Long-term and Short-term)

Long-term goals: promote recognition of women role models, increase productivity and high-quality feedback for professional development, strengthen front-line leadership in computational biology, and foster a collaborative community where solidarity, gender equality, and excellence are the norm.

Short-term goals: initiate a one-year pilot scheme, train mentors and mentees, build mentoring relationships beyond the mentee's immediate field, maximize mentees' potential toward senior positions, evaluate the pilot, and plan future editions including support for postdoctoral researchers transitioning to independent careers.

Programme Structure, Timeline, and Outcomes

The pilot included mentor training, mentee preparation, a matching algorithm for mentor-mentee pairing, individual meetings led by mentees, regular follow-up sessions, and wrap-up evaluation. The structure integrated training in gender dimension in research and practical tools such as non-biased job description writing.

Poster-reported outcomes showed strong satisfaction among participants, including high ratings for overall satisfaction, workshop content, and training applicability. The programme also reported positive mentor benefits such as increased creativity, networking opportunities, and expanded role profile, while mentees highlighted wider professional networks and career development support.

Who Is the Pilot For?

The programme was designed for both mentors and mentees. Mentors are female leaders and allies advocating for gender equality in science; mentees are young scientists, with the pilot including at least 70% women participants.

Benefits highlighted in the programme materials include career development for mentors, fresh ideas and personal satisfaction, and for mentees, access to wider professional networks, improved skills and aptitude, and stronger career opportunities through interaction with experienced scientists.
